The Mughal Empire

1526 – 1857Capitals: Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Delhi

Founded by Babur after the First Battle of Panipat, the Mughal empire grew under Akbar and Shah Jahan into the greatest power of the subcontinent, endured Aurangzeb's endless Deccan wars, then declined through the 18th century until it ended with Bahadur Shah Zafar in 1858.

The Great Mughals

B

Babur

1526 – 1530

Defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat (1526), then Khanwa (1527) and the Ghaghra (1529); founded the empire and wrote the Baburnama.

H

Humayun

1530 – 1540, 1555 – 1556

Lost the throne to Sher Shah Suri at Chausa and Kannauj; regained Delhi in 1555 with Safavid help, but died months later.

A

Akbar

1556 – 1605

Won the Second Battle of Panipat (1556); built the Mansabdari system, allied with the Rajputs, founded Fatehpur Sikri and annexed Malwa, Gujarat, Bengal and the Deccan.

J

Jahangir

1605 – 1627

Set up the “chain of justice” at Agra; Nur Jahan's influence grew; lost Kandahar to Persia (1622); a great patron of painting.

SJ

Shah Jahan

1628 – 1658

The builder-emperor: Taj Mahal, Red Fort and Jama Masjid of Delhi; pushed campaigns into the Deccan; deposed by Aurangzeb in 1658.

A

Aurangzeb

1658 – 1707

Expanded the empire to its largest extent; annexed Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687); reimposed jizya (1679); executed Sambhaji (1689); the Deccan wars drained his treasury.

Later Mughals (after Aurangzeb)

BS

Bahadur Shah I

1707 – 1712

Won the succession war; authority faded fast; died during a Sind campaign.

JS

Jahandar Shah

1712 – 1713

First puppet emperor, raised and deposed by the Sayyid brothers.

F

Farrukhsiyar

1713 – 1719

Granted the East India Company duty-free trade in Bengal (1717); killed by the Sayyids.

MS

Muhammad Shah

1719 – 1748

Saw Nadir Shah's invasion and the sack of Delhi (1739); the empire shrank on every side.

AS

Ahmad Shah

1748 – 1754

Faced Ahmad Shah Abdali's raids; blinded and deposed.

A

Alamgir II

1754 – 1759

A puppet of the wazirs; Abdali sacked Delhi again (1757).

SA

Shah Alam II

1759 – 1806

Confirmed the Company's Diwani of Bengal (1765); blinded by Ghulam Qadir (1788); ended his days as a British pensioner.

A2

Akbar II

1806 – 1837

Emperor in name only; conferred the title “Raja” on Ram Mohan Roy.

BZ

Bahadur Shah Zafar

1837 – 1857

The last emperor; figurehead of the 1857 revolt; exiled to Rangoon, where he died in 1862. The dynasty was formally abolished in 1858.

Major Events

  1. 1526

    First Battle of Panipat

    Babur's artillery and tactics crush Ibrahim Lodi's far larger army — the Mughal empire is born.

  2. 1539-40

    Chausa & Kannauj

    Sher Shah Suri defeats Humayun twice, driving him into Persian exile and creating the Sur interregnum.

  3. 1556

    Second Battle of Panipat

    Akbar's forces under Bairam Khan defeat Hemu, securing the Mughal restoration after Humayun's death.

  4. 1576

    Battle of Haldighati

    Akbar's army defeats Maharana Pratap, though Mewar's resistance continues for years.

  5. 1632-53

    The Taj Mahal

    Shah Jahan raises the marble mausoleum at Agra for Mumtaz Mahal — the empire's most enduring monument.

  6. 1689

    Sambhaji executed

    Aurangzeb's killing of Shivaji's son ignites the 26-year Maratha war of attrition in the Deccan.

  7. 1739

    Nadir Shah sacks Delhi

    The Persian invader carries off the Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor at the Battle of Karnal; the Mughals never recover.

  8. 1857-58

    The Last Mughal

    Bahadur Shah Zafar becomes the reluctant figurehead of the great revolt; tried and exiled as the empire is abolished.

The Maratha Confederacy

1674 – 1818Capitals: Raigad & Satara (Chhatrapatis), Pune (Peshwas)

Shivaji built a kingdom of “swarajya” in the Sahyadri hills; after his death the Marathas fought a 27-year war with Aurangzeb and rose again under the Peshwas to dominate 18th-century India, until the Anglo-Maratha Wars left the Company paramount in 1818.

The Chhatrapatis

S

Shivaji

1674 – 1680

Crowned Chhatrapati at Raigad (1674); killed Afzal Khan (1659); made his legendary escape from Agra (1666); built the Maratha state and navy.

S

Sambhaji

1680 – 1689

Fought Aurangzeb's full-scale invasion; captured, tortured and executed (1689) — turning the Deccan into a graveyard for Mughal armies.

R

Rajaram

1689 – 1700

Kept the resistance alive from the fortress of Jinji in the far south; his commanders wore down the Mughals fortress by fortress.

T

Tarabai

1700 – 1707

Regent for the young Shivaji II; a fierce war-leader who carried the Maratha defence through the hardest years of the 27-year war.

S

Shahu

1707 – 1749

Released by the Mughals and won the succession struggle; made Satara his seat and empowered the Peshwas, opening the era of Maratha expansion.

The Peshwas (prime ministers who became real power)

BV

Balaji Vishwanath

1713 – 1720

The first Peshwa; won recognition and revenue rights (chauth and sardeshmukhi) from the Mughal court, integrating the Marathas into imperial politics.

BR

Baji Rao I

1720 – 1740

The greatest Peshwa, never defeated in battle; beat the Nizam at Palkhed (1728) and Bhopal (1737); expanded into Malwa, Gujarat, Bundelkhand — and raided Delhi itself.

BB

Balaji Baji Rao

1740 – 1761

Presided over the confederacy's greatest reach — and its greatest disaster: Ahmad Shah Abdali's victory at Panipat (1761). He died broken soon after.

MR

Madhav Rao I

1761 – 1772

The “restorer”: within a decade he rebuilt Maratha power after Panipat, regained lost territory and defeated every rival, north and south.

B2

Baji Rao II

1796 – 1818

The last Peshwa; the Treaty of Bassein (1802) made him a British dependent, and defeat in the Anglo-Maratha Wars ended his rule in 1818.

Major Events

  1. 1666

    Escape from Agra

    Shivaji's daring escape from Aurangzeb's captivity turns him into a legend across India.

  2. 1674

    Coronation at Raigad

    Shivaji is crowned Chhatrapati — a sovereign Hindu king in defiance of Mughal authority.

  3. 1689-1707

    The 27-Year War

    From Sambhaji's execution to Aurangzeb's death, Maratha commanders and fortresses bleed the Mughal army dry in the Deccan.

  4. 1728

    Battle of Palkhed

    Baji Rao I outmanoeuvres the Nizam, establishing Maratha supremacy in the Deccan.

  5. 1737

    Delhi Raided

    Baji Rao I appears at the gates of Delhi — the Mughal capital has no answer to Maratha cavalry.

  6. 1761

    Third Battle of Panipat

    Ahmad Shah Abdali destroys the great Maratha army; the confederacy is shattered but rebuilds within a decade.

  7. 1802

    Treaty of Bassein

    Baji Rao II accepts British protection, triggering the Second Anglo-Maratha War.

  8. 1818

    The End of Maratha Power

    The Third Anglo-Maratha War ends; the Peshwa is dethroned and exiled to Bithur, and the Company rules India.

The Nawabs of Awadh

1722 – 1856Capitals: Faizabad, then Lucknow

The richest of the Mughal successor states, Awadh grew from a governorship into a kingdom whose court at Lucknow became famous for music, poetry and grand architecture. Constricted step by step by the East India Company, it was annexed in 1856 — and became the heartland of the revolt a year later.

Nawabs and Kings

SK

Saadat Ali Khan I

1722 – 1739

Founder of the Awadh dynasty (Burhan-ul-Mulk); a capable Mughal governor who made his province effectively independent.

SJ

Safdarjung

1739 – 1754

Also wielded power at Delhi as the Mughal wazir; a major patron of architecture.

SD

Shuja-ud-Daula

1754 – 1775

Fought the Company at Buxar (1764) and lost; the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) put Awadh under British “protection” as a buffer state.

AD

Asaf-ud-Daula

1775 – 1797

Moved the capital to Lucknow and gave it its glitter; built the Bara Imambara (1784), employing thousands during a famine.

WA

Wazir Ali Khan

1797 – 1798

A brief, turbulent reign; deposed with British approval in months.

S2

Saadat Ali Khan II

1798 – 1814

The Subsidiary Alliance (1801) ceded Rohilkhand and the Lower Doab and halved the army; the British Resident's word became law.

GD

Ghazi-ud-Din Haidar

1814 – 1827

Upgraded his title to King of Awadh (1819) — grandeur couldn't mask dependence on the Company.

ND

Nasir-ud-Din Haidar

1827 – 1837

Continued Lucknow's building boom and lavish court patronage.

MA

Muhammad Ali Shah

1837 – 1842

Built the Chhota Imambara and the Satkhanda; famous for his generosity to shrines.

AA

Amjad Ali Shah

1842 – 1847

A short reign under mounting British pressure.

W

Wajid Ali Shah

1847 – 1856

Poet, musician and thumri patron; deposed and exiled to Calcutta when Dalhousie annexed Awadh in February 1856.

After the Annexation

In 1857, Wajid Ali Shah's wife Begum Hazrat Mahal led the revolt in Lucknow in the name of their young son Birjis Qadr. Her forces recaptured the city and held out until 1858 — the last battle for an independent Awadh.

Major Events

  1. 1722

    The Dynasty Begins

    Saadat Ali Khan takes charge of Awadh and turns it into an autonomous state in all but name.

  2. 1764

    Battle of Buxar

    Shuja-ud-Daula, Mir Qasim and Shah Alam II are crushed by the Company — the turning point for North India.

  3. 1765

    Treaty of Allahabad

    Awadh becomes a British-protected buffer, its territories restored in exchange for a huge subsidy.

  4. 1775

    Lucknow Becomes the Capital

    Asaf-ud-Daula moves the court, beginning Lucknow's golden age of arts and architecture.

  5. 1784

    Bara Imambara

    Built as famine relief for thousands of workers — and still one of India's grandest halls without a single supporting beam.

  6. 1801

    Subsidiary Alliance

    Saadat Ali Khan II cedes half the kingdom and cuts his army to avoid British attack.

  7. 1856

    Annexation

    Dalhousie seizes Awadh on the pretext of misrule; Wajid Ali Shah is exiled — vast resentment follows.

  8. 1857-58

    Begum Hazrat Mahal's Revolt

    Lucknow rises; the Begum's government holds the city until its final capture in 1858.

The Nawabs of Bengal

1717 – 1772Capital: Murshidabad

Bengal — the richest province of the Mughal empire — became virtually independent under its first Nawab, Murshid Quli Khan. Its defeat at Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) handed the Company the revenues of the wealthiest region in the world, opening the road to British rule in India.

The Nawabs

MQ

Murshid Quli Khan

1717 – 1727

Founder of the nawabi; his revenue reforms made Bengal the empire's most orderly and prosperous province.

SD

Shuja-ud-Din Muhammad Khan

1727 – 1739

A peaceful, prosperous reign — Bengal kept aloof from the storms consuming Delhi.

SF

Sarfaraz Khan

1739 – 1740

Deposed and killed by his own deputy, Alivardi Khan, in a swift succession struggle.

AL

Alivardi Khan

1740 – 1756

Fended off a decade of Maratha (Bargi) raids, ceding Orissa to buy peace; a strong ruler whose death left a young, untested heir.

SU

Siraj-ud-Daulah

1756 – 1757

Attacked and took Calcutta (1756) — the episode remembered as the “Black Hole”; defeated by Clive at Plassey (1757) and killed.

MJ

Mir Jafar

1757 – 1760, 1763 – 1765

His betrayal at Plassey made him Nawab and the Company the real power; squeezed for money, he was deposed and briefly restored.

MQ2

Mir Qasim

1760 – 1763

Tried to reclaim independence: moved his capital to Munger and abolished internal trade duties — the Company answered with war and Buxar (1764).

NJ

Najm-ud-Daulah

1765 – 1766

A boy Nawab under the Company's new Diwani; after him the nawabs were pensioners in all but name.

After 1765

With the Diwani, the East India Company collected Bengal's revenues while the nawabs remained ceremonial rulers at Murshidabad. In 1772 Warren Hastings ended the “dual government” and brought Bengal directly under Company administration — the beginning of British India.

Major Events

  1. 1717

    The Nawabi Begins

    Murshid Quli Khan is recognised as Nawab Nazim of Bengal — independence in all but name.

  2. 1742-51

    The Maratha Raids

    Alivardi Khan fights off the Bargis; Bengal pays a heavy price for peace.

  3. 1756

    The Black Hole of Calcutta

    Siraj-ud-Daulah seizes Calcutta; the alleged night of prisoners in a small cell becomes Company propaganda for revenge.

  4. 1757

    Battle of Plassey

    Clive's small force wins because Mir Jafar and others never fight — Bengal's treasury passes to the Company.

  5. 1764

    Battle of Buxar

    Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II are beaten decisively — the Company becomes a territorial power.

  6. 1765

    The Diwani

    Shah Alam II grants the Company the revenue rights of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; puppet nawabs rule on a British leash.

  7. 1772

    End of the Dual Government

    Warren Hastings takes direct charge of Bengal — the nawabi survives only as ceremony.

Jhansi & Rani Lakshmi Bai

1742 – 1858Capital: Jhansi (Bundelkhand)

A small Bundelkhand state ruled by the Newalkar house under Maratha suzerainty became famous through one extraordinary year of courage. When the Doctrine of Lapse took Jhansi from its widowed rani in 1853, Lakshmi Bai became the most celebrated warrior of 1857 — dying in battle at just thirty.

The Newalkar House

RN

Raghunath Rao Newalkar I

mid-1700s

Established the Newalkar family's authority at Jhansi under Maratha (Chhatrapati and Peshwa) suzerainty.

SH

Shiv Rao Harihar Rao

late 1700s

Consolidated the state's territory; Jhansi grew into a fortified town and trade centre of Bundelkhand.

R2

Raghunath Rao II

early 1800s

Ruled as British power closed in around the princely states of Bundelkhand.

GR

Gangadhar Rao Newalkar

1843 – 1853

Married Manikarnika (Manu) in 1842; on his deathbed adopted a son, Damodar Rao — an adoption the British refused to recognise.

The Rani (1828 – 1858)

LB

Rani Lakshmi Bai

Rani of Jhansi, 1853 – 1858

Born Manikarnika at Varanasi (1828), trained in horse-riding and sword-fighting. When Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse annexed Jhansi in 1854, she vowed to win it back. In 1857 she took charge of the defense of Jhansi, held the fort for two weeks under Hugh Rose's bombardment in 1858, escaped through the siege lines, and fell fighting in the recapture of Gwalior on 18 June 1858.

Major Events

  1. 1828

    Birth at Varanasi

    Manikarnika, later called Manu and then Lakshmi Bai, is born on 19 November.

  2. 1842

    Marriage to the Raja

    She marries Raja Gangadhar Rao of Jhansi and becomes its rani.

  3. 1851

    A Son Lost

    Their infant son dies — after which the couple adopt Damodar Rao.

  4. 1853-54

    Doctrine of Lapse

    Gangadhar Rao dies; the British refuse to accept the adoption, annex Jhansi and cut the rani's pension. Her appeals fail.

  5. 1857

    The Revolt Reaches Jhansi

    The garrison rises in June; the Rani takes charge of the state in Damodar Rao's name.

  6. 1858

    Siege of Jhansi

    March–April: Hugh Rose's Central India Field Force bombards the fort. The Rani escapes by night to Kalpi, then joins Tatya Tope's strike on Gwalior.

  7. 1858

    Death at Gwalior

    On 18 June, in armour and on horseback, she falls in the fighting near Phool Bagh — remembered ever after as the Rani of Jhansi.
