The people

Those who made the era

Queens and emperors, reformers and revolutionaries, lawyers and engineers. No list can be complete — but these are some of the figures who shaped India after 1857.

The revolt Reformers Freedom movement The Republic

Voices of the revolt

BZ

Bahadur Shah Zafar

1775–1862Mughal emperor

The last Mughal emperor and the revolt's symbolic leader. Exiled to Rangoon after 1858, he died there in 1862.

RL

Rani Lakshmibai

1828–1858Queen of Jhansi

Fought to restore her adopted son's claim after annexation. Her death in battle at Gwalior made her the uprising's most enduring symbol.

NS

Nana Saheb

1824–c.1859Peshwa claimant

Led the Kanpur uprising after being denied his pension. His fate after 1859 remains unknown.

TT

Tantia Tope

1814–1859General

Nana Saheb's general and a brilliant guerrilla commander who fought across central India until his capture and execution.

HM

Begum Hazrat Mahal

c.1820–1879Regent of Awadh

Organised resistance in Lucknow, refused amnesty, and died in exile in Nepal.

KS

Kunwar Singh

1777–1858Zamindar of Jagdishpur

The octogenarian zamindar who led a formidable army in Bihar and died of wounds shortly after his final victory.

Reformers and thinkers

RR

Raja Ram Mohan Roy

1772–1833Reformer

A founder of the Brahmo Sabha and a campaigner against sati, he is often called the father of the Indian Renaissance.

IV

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar

1820–1891Reformer and educator

His campaign helped pass the Widow Remarriage Act of 1856. He also promoted women's education and simplified Bengali prose.

JP

Jyotirao Phule

1827–1890Anti-caste activist

Founded the Satyashodhak Samaj and fought caste oppression and Brahminical authority in Maharashtra.

SP

Savitribai Phule

1831–1897Educator and poet

Opened one of India's first schools for girls in 1848 and wrote against caste and gender injustice.

SV

Swami Vivekananda

1863–1902Monk and thinker

His 1893 address to the Parliament of Religions in Chicago made him a global figure. He founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897.

RT

Rabindranath Tagore

1861–1941Poet and educator

Winner of the 1913 Nobel Prize in Literature, founder of Santiniketan, and author of India's national anthem.

The freedom movement

DN

Dadabhai Naoroji

1825–1917Nationalist and economist

Author of the drain theory and the first Indian elected to the British House of Commons (1892). Thrice Congress president.

GG

Gopal Krishna Gokhale

1866–1915Moderate leader

Founder of the Servants of India Society and Gandhi's political mentor, he championed constitutional reform and education.

BT

Bal Gangadhar Tilak

1856–1920Extremist leader

The “Lokmanya” whose newspapers and mass festivals made nationalism popular. He declared: “Swaraj is my birthright.”

LR

Lala Lajpat Rai

1865–1928Punjab leader

A member of Lal-Bal-Pal and a fearless critic of the Raj, he died after a police lathi-charge during protests against the Simon Commission.

BP

Bipin Chandra Pal

1858–1932Swadeshi theorist

A leading voice of the Swadeshi movement and one of the Lal-Bal-Pal trio, he argued for self-reliance and mass action.

MG

Mahatma Gandhi

1869–1948Leader of the mass movement

Turned the Congress into a mass organisation and led Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience and Quit India. Full profile →

MJ

Muhammad Ali Jinnah

1876–1948Founder of Pakistan

Began as a Congress moderate, became the Muslim League's leader, and led the demand for a separate Pakistan. First Governor-General of Pakistan.

JN

Jawaharlal Nehru

1889–1964First Prime Minister

Congress president, architect of non-alignment, and India's first Prime Minister (1947–64), he shaped the republic's democratic and industrial vision.

SB

Subhas Chandra Bose

1897–1945Leader of the INA

The “Netaji” who broke with the Congress, sought Axis support and built the Indian National Army to fight the Raj.

BS

Bhagat Singh

1907–1931Revolutionary

A socialist revolutionary and the most famous figure of the HSRA, hanged in Lahore at the age of 23.

VP

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

1875–1950Deputy Prime Minister

The “Iron Man” who, with V. P. Menon, integrated more than 560 princely states into the Indian Union.

SN

Sarojini Naidu

1879–1949Poet and leader

The “Nightingale of India,” the first Indian woman to preside over the Congress (1925), and India's first woman governor.

MA

Maulana Abul Kalam Azad

1888–1958Congress leader

A Congress president during the freedom struggle and India's first Education Minister, he championed secularism and institution-building.

BA

B. R. Ambedkar

1891–1956Constitution-maker

Chair of the Drafting Committee, champion of Dalit rights, and the central architect of the Indian Constitution.

Builders of the Republic

RP

Rajendra Prasad

1884–1963First President

President of the Constituent Assembly and the first President of India, serving from 1950 to 1962.

LS

Lal Bahadur Shastri

1904–1966Prime Minister

Guided India through the 1965 war and gave the slogan “Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan.” He died at Tashkent in 1966.

IG

Indira Gandhi

1917–1984Prime Minister

India's first woman Prime Minister, victor of the 1971 war, and the central figure of the Emergency. Assassinated in 1984.

AK

A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

1931–2015Scientist and President

The “Missile Man” who led India's missile programmes and served as the eleventh President (2002–07).

AV

Atal Bihari Vajpayee

1924–2018Prime Minister

A poet-politician who ordered the Pokhran-II tests and led India through the Kargil War as Prime Minister (1998–2004).

MS

Manmohan Singh

1932–2024Economist and Prime Minister

As Finance Minister in 1991, he launched the reforms that opened India's economy; he later served as Prime Minister from 2004 to 2014.
