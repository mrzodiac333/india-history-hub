Timeline of Reform

1772

Raja Ram Mohan Roy born — the "Father of the Indian Renaissance" who would go on to abolish Sati.

1820

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar born — scholar and campaigner for widow remarriage.

1824

Dayanand Saraswati born — founder of the Arya Samaj and the "Back to the Vedas" movement.

1827

Jyotiba Phule born — pioneer of women's education and anti-caste reform.

1828

Brahmo Samaj founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in Calcutta, marking the beginning of organised socio-religious reform.

1829

Bengal Sati Regulation — Sati officially banned, largely due to Roy's campaign.

1831

Savitribai Phule born — India's first female teacher.

1848

First school for girls in India founded by Jyotiba and Savitribai Phule in Pune.

1856

Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act passed, following Vidyasagar's sustained campaign.

1863

Swami Vivekananda born — spiritual leader who took Vedanta to the West.

1869

Mahatma Gandhi born — the apostle of non-violence who would lead India to freedom.

1875

Arya Samaj founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in Bombay.

1879

Sarojini Naidu born — poet, freedom fighter, and first woman governor of India.

1882

Subramania Bharati born — revolutionary Tamil poet who stirred nationalist fervour.

1885

Indian National Congress founded — the platform through which many reformers would fight for freedom.

1891

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar born — jurist, social reformer, and chief architect of the Indian Constitution.

1893

Swami Vivekananda's historic address at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago.

1905

Partition of Bengal — sparks the Swadeshi movement led by Lala Lajpat Rai and others.

1907

Bhagat Singh born — the revolutionary martyr who became an enduring symbol of resistance.

1920

Non-Cooperation Movement launched by Gandhi — the first mass struggle for independence.

1928

Lala Lajpat Rai martyred during a protest against the Simon Commission.

1930

Dandi March — Gandhi's salt satyagraha; Sarojini Naidu leads Dharasana Satyagraha.

1931

Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev executed by the British.

1947

India gains independence. Sarojini Naidu becomes first woman governor.

1950

Indian Constitution adopted — Dr. Ambedkar's vision enshrined in law.

1956

Dr. Ambedkar converts to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of followers, sparking a mass Dalit Buddhist movement.
