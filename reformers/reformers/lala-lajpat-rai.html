Lala Lajpat Rai

28 January 1865 – 17 November 1928

The Lion of Punjab

Known for: Leadership in Swadeshi movement, founding Punjab National Bank, educational institutions, martyrdom

Early Life

Born in Dhudike, Punjab, Lajpat Rai studied law and established a successful practice in Hissar. Deeply influenced by the Arya Samaj, he became a prominent figure in the Hindu reform movement and Indian nationalism.

National Leader

He was one of the "Lal-Bal-Pal" triumvirate (with Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal) that advocated assertive nationalism. He led the Swadeshi movement in Punjab, promoting Indian industries and boycotting British goods.

Education & Banking

Lajpat Rai founded the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV) College in Lahore and was instrumental in establishing the Punjab National Bank (1894), one of India's oldest banks. He believed economic self-reliance was essential for political freedom.

Martyrdom

On 30 October 1928, Lajpat Rai led a protest against the Simon Commission in Lahore. He was brutally lathi-charged by police. The injuries led to his death on 17 November 1928. His famous last words: "Every blow struck on my body will prove a nail in the coffin of the British Empire."

Legacy

Lala Lajpat Rai is remembered as a fearless patriot who combined social reform with political activism. His contributions to education and banking continue to benefit millions. His death sparked a new wave of revolutionary fervor, inspiring Bhagat Singh and others.
