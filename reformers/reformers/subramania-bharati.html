Subramania Bharati

11 December 1882 – 12 September 1921

The Poet of Revolution

Known for: Tamil poetry, Indian independence activism, social reform through literature

Early Life

Born in Ettayapuram, Tamil Nadu, Bharati was a gifted poet from childhood. He worked as a journalist for the Tamil daily Swadeshamitran and later edited the India weekly, using the press as a weapon against British rule.

Poetry & Patriotism

Bharati's poetry ignited the spirit of nationalism in South India. His songs like "Vande Mataram," "Swaraj Song," and "India's Freedom" became anthems of the freedom movement. He wrote in simple, powerful Tamil that reached the masses.

Social Vision

He dreamed of a casteless, classless, and gender-equal India. He wrote passionately against caste discrimination, child marriage, and the oppression of women. He envisioned "Bharat Mata" as a mother to all her children equally.

Exile & Return

In 1908, facing arrest, Bharati fled to Pondicherry (French India) where he lived in exile for 10 years. He continued writing and editing revolutionary publications. He returned to British India in 1918 and was imprisoned shortly after.

Legacy

Subramania Bharati is considered the father of modern Tamil poetry. His works continue to inspire Tamil literature and the cultural identity of Tamil Nadu. His birthday is celebrated as Bharati Day in Tamil Nadu.
