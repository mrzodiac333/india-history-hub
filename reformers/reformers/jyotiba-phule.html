Jyotiba Phule

11 April 1827 – 28 November 1890

Champion of the Oppressed

Known for: Women's education, anti-caste movement, Satyashodhak Samaj

Early Life

Born in Satara, Maharashtra, to a family of the Mali (gardener) caste, Phule was inspired by Thomas Paine's Rights of Man. He saw education as the key to liberation and devoted his life to uplifting the lower castes and women.

Schools for All

In 1848, Phule and his wife Savitribai opened the first school for girls in India. They went on to establish multiple schools for children from marginalized communities, facing fierce opposition from orthodox society.

Satyashodhak Samaj

In 1873, he founded the Satyashodhak Samaj (Society of Seekers of Truth) to promote social equality, rational thinking, and the rejection of Brahminical dominance. The movement empowered Shudras and Ati-Shudras to claim their rights.

Writings

His book Gulamgiri (Slavery, 1873) was a powerful critique of the caste system, dedicated to the African American abolitionist movement. He also wrote plays and poems exposing social hypocrisy.

Legacy

Jyotiba Phule is remembered as one of India's most radical social reformers. His vision of education for all and dignity for the oppressed deeply influenced later movements, including Dr. Ambedkar's struggle for Dalit rights.
