Swami Vivekananda

12 January 1863 – 4 July 1902

The Monk Who Introduced Vedanta to the West

Known for: Historic 1893 Chicago speech, founding Ramakrishna Mission, preaching Vedanta

Early Life

Born Narendranath Datta in Calcutta, he was a bright, restless youth drawn to spirituality. He found his guru in Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, who transformed him from a skeptical intellectual into a powerful monk named Vivekananda.

Chicago Address

On 11 September 1893 at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago, Vivekananda began his speech with "Sisters and brothers of America," earning a standing ovation. He spoke of universal tolerance and the harmony of religions, putting India's spiritual heritage on the global stage.

Teachings

He taught that every soul is potentially divine, that service to humanity is service to God, and that all religions lead to the same truth. He urged Indians to have faith in themselves: "Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached."

Ramakrishna Mission

Founded in 1897, the Ramakrishna Mission combines spiritual teaching with social service. It runs hospitals, schools, colleges, and relief work across India and the world.

Legacy

Swami Vivekananda inspired millions worldwide. His birthday is celebrated as National Youth Day in India. His words continue to motivate generations to strive for excellence, spiritual growth, and national unity.
