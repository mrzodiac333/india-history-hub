Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar

26 September 1820 – 29 July 1891

The Ocean of Knowledge

Known for: Widow remarriage movement, education reform, Bengali prose simplification

Early Life

Born in Birsingha, Bengal, Ishwar Chandra was a child prodigy. He earned the title "Vidyasagar" (Ocean of Knowledge) for his vast learning. He became the head pandit of Fort William College and later principal of Sanskrit College, where he opened admissions to non-Brahmins.

Widow Remarriage

His greatest social reform was the campaign for Hindu widow remarriage. He published tracts, gathered evidence from ancient scriptures, and petitioned the government. His efforts led to the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act of 1856, one of the most progressive laws of the time.

Education Reformer

Vidyasagar revolutionized Bengali education. He simplified the Bengali alphabet and prose, wrote textbooks, and established over 20 schools for girls across Bengal. He believed universal education was essential for national progress.

Philanthropy

Known for his immense generosity, Vidyasagar donated most of his earnings to charity. He supported the poor, widows, and students, often giving away his last rupee. He lived a life of extreme simplicity despite his fame.

Legacy

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar is revered as a symbol of compassion and enlightenment. His reforms transformed Bengali society and inspired subsequent generations of social reformers across India.
